# AI handoff: D8D Tech identity and pre-launch website

Prepared 26 September 2026. This is a self-contained creative and implementation brief, not public website copy. It reflects the latest confirmed planning status; it is not evidence of a completed incorporation or launch.

## 1. Your assignment

Create a coherent, original identity and a polished, working pre-launch website for **D8D Tech**:

1. Design an original logo and export usable assets.
2. Produce practical brand guidelines and reusable design tokens.
3. Build a responsive website that applies the identity consistently.
4. Supply local run/build instructions and a clear delivery note.

Do the design and implementation, not just a moodboard, prompt or written proposal. The repository is intentionally empty of application code so you can choose a proportionate technical approach.

The immediate outcome is a **local prototype for owner review**, not a public launch, online store or finished legal identity. Treat the strongest design as your recommendation, not a claim that the owner has approved the brand.

## 3. Brand idea and creative freedom

The requested thematic starting point is **second chances and new beginnings**, expressed subtly through giving useful devices another chapter. "Day Eight" is the new-beginning idea behind the name. It is a creative theme, not a religious claim about the owner or a reason to add overt religious imagery.

Use **Day Eight Devices** as the underlying phrase. Do not expand it to "Day Eight Devices Technologies". No tagline, palette, typography or logo has been approved.

Desired impression: trustworthy, clear, modern, approachable and commercially credible. Prefer a distinctive, considered identity over a generic electronics reseller template or exaggerated luxury positioning.

Creative directions worth exploring include the D/8/D letterforms, a subtle transition, continuity or a renewed cycle. These are suggestions, not mandatory motifs. Avoid relying on a generic recycling symbol, a copied infinity logo, a phone icon with stock lettering, or environmental symbolism that implies unproven certification.

Present two or three concise visual directions, recommend one and develop that recommendation into the full asset set and site. Keep the alternatives brief; the useful deliverable is the finished recommended direction. Do not stop awaiting approval of every reversible design detail.

The private business-launch dashboard in the parent project is an internal planning tool, not the approved public brand. Do not copy its contents or assume its palette is mandatory.

## 4. Logo deliverables

Deliver editable vector originals, not only raster previews or a screenshot:

- Primary D8D Tech wordmark/lockup, an alternate compact or stacked arrangement, and a standalone symbol where the design supports one.
- Full-colour, one-colour, dark-on-light and light-on-dark versions.
- Clean SVG exports with sensible view boxes, no embedded scripts, and no dependency on a remote font to render correctly.
- Transparent PNG exports at practical sizes, plus a favicon set suitable for the website.
- A presentation showing the mark on light/dark backgrounds and at small sizes.
- A short rationale explaining the selected idea and how it relates to the brand without inventing a company history.

Preserve an editable design source and provide distribution exports with lettering that renders reliably. Check legibility at favicon size and as a small website header logo. Avoid fine details that disappear.

Do not imitate Apple, Samsung, marketplace logos or the existing D8D-root technology brands. Do not use a registration symbol or claim the logo/name has trademark protection.

## 5. Brand-guideline deliverables

Create a readable `BRAND-GUIDELINES.md` and a visual reference board, such as a local HTML page or image export. Include:

- Positioning, personality and tone of voice; distinguish intended values from proven operational promises.
- Logo variants, clear-space rule, minimum sizes, backgrounds and misuse examples.
- Primary/secondary/neutral colours with HEX and RGB values and their intended roles.
- Tested accessible text/background pairings; do not label every palette combination accessible.
- Typography, hierarchy, fallback fonts and licensing/source details.
- Spacing, layout widths, corner radii, borders, iconography and motion principles.
- Button, link, navigation and focus-state examples consistent with the website.
- Imagery direction and asset-licensing rules.
- Short copy examples and any proposed taglines clearly labelled as recommendations.
- Corresponding CSS variables or equivalent design tokens actually used by the implementation.

Make the identity reusable for later business cards, packaging and trade documents, but do not create stationery containing invented legal/contact details.

## 6. Website scope

Prefer one excellent marketing page over a large unfinished site. Use additional pages only where they materially improve the result.

Suggested content structure:

| Section | Purpose and boundaries |
|---|---|
| Header | Original identity and simple navigation; no fabricated account/shop controls. |
| Hero | Explain what D8D Tech is preparing to do. Include a visible pre-launch statement, not just a footer disclaimer. |
| The idea | A concise explanation of Day Eight Devices and continued device use; no invented founding story or operational track record. |
| Our intended approach | Explain planned testing, clear grading, data erasure and careful handling as arrangements being developed, not completed certifications or guarantees. |
| Future buyers and partners | Indicate the intended consumer/trade audience without promising a country, platform, supplier relationship or shipping service. |
| Launch status / contact | State that launch and contact details will follow. No functioning contact form or invented email address. |
| Footer | Accurate pre-launch identity, navigation and any necessary attribution; no invented company number, VAT number, registered office or legal accreditation. |

Adapt this structure if a better design emerges; preserve its factual boundaries.

Suggested factual copy seed:

> D8D Tech is a used-phone resale business in preparation in Ireland. We are developing our sourcing, testing and sales arrangements. We are not currently accepting orders, payments or devices.

This is a safe starting point, not mandatory final headline copy. The copy can be more engaging without changing its meaning.

Use honest, working calls to action, such as an anchor to "Explore our approach". A pre-launch page can still be visually complete without a lead form. If contact details are unavailable, present that plainly rather than adding a fake button or `hello@...` address.

### Explicitly out of scope for this version

- Product inventory, model prices, availability, "shop now", carts, checkout, payments or reservations.
- Newsletter/waitlist collection, contact forms, WhatsApp integrations, CRM, accounts or customer uploads.
- Analytics, advertising pixels, tracking cookies, live chat and unnecessary third-party embeds.
- Automatic emails, external submissions, hosting accounts or deployment.
- Statements implying an approved warehouse, shipping network, insurance, certification or regulatory clearance.
- Invented reviews, customer logos, sales figures, years of experience, partnerships, test reports or team profiles.
- Quantified carbon savings, "zero waste", guaranteed battery health, warranty/returns promises or certification claims without evidence and owner approval.
- Blanket "GDPR compliant", "fully compliant" or similar assurances.

These restrictions apply to metadata, image captions, alt text, structured data and social preview text as well as visible copy. Do not add Product/Offer/review structured data for nonexistent stock or a fictional incorporated Organisation profile.

## 7. Technical expectations

Choose the simplest maintainable stack that can deliver the design well. A static implementation or a small static-capable framework is appropriate; a database, authentication system, payment service and complex CMS are not needed.

- Keep branding, contact/status copy and reusable tokens easy to update when the name and company details are final.
- Use semantic HTML, keyboard-accessible navigation, visible focus, appropriate headings and descriptive accessible names.
- Aim for WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, reflow/zoom, motion preferences and usable touch targets. Report what you tested rather than claiming certification.
- Design for narrow phones, tablets and desktop screens; no horizontal page overflow or unreadably small text.
- Optimise images and fonts. Prefer properly licensed local assets and avoid runtime third-party requests by default.
- Use original illustrations or licensed imagery. Decorative phone imagery must not imply photographed owned inventory.
- Keep all actual controls working. Avoid dead links, fake submissions and nonfunctional menu items.
- Test loading, navigation, responsiveness and the browser console. Add the proportionate checks supported by your chosen implementation.
- If you introduce dependencies, commit the appropriate lockfile and document supported runtime/package-manager versions and commands.
- Keep secrets out of the repository. Any future `.env.example` must contain placeholders only.
- Keep the prototype local. Add an appropriate no-index posture for any future review build, but remember that `noindex` and `robots.txt` are not access control. Public hosting still needs approval; private shared previews need suitable authentication/access restrictions.

Do not add a public canonical URL, production domain, real form endpoint or analytics identifier by guessing it. Do not label the prototype production-ready while factual or publication gates remain.
