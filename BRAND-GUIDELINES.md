# D8D Tech — provisional brand guidelines

> **Status:** This is a proposed identity for owner review, prepared for a local pre-launch prototype. **D8D Tech** is a working name for **Day Eight Devices**; neither the name nor the identity is presented as legally cleared, adopted, registered or protected.

## 1. Recommended direction: Dawn Loop

**Dawn Loop** is the recommended direction and the one developed in the website and export set. It combines crisp, Apple-adjacent restraint—ample space, precise proportions, a quiet dark-to-light palette—with a subtle idea of renewal. The mark places a teal `8` between two D forms: a continuous middle, held by simple bookends; the tightly spaced `Tech` lettering keeps the lockup direct and commercially clear.

The underlying Day Eight idea is **a new beginning**. Its light Christian resonance is deliberately quiet: no crosses, verses, promises of ministry, or claims about the owner’s beliefs. It should feel like a considered second chapter for useful technology, not a charity, church, recycling certification or environmental campaign.

### Concise explored directions

| Direction | Idea | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dawn Loop** | D–8–D forms, a repeated loop and a tightly kerned Tech lockup. Calm, technically precise and quietly optimistic. | **Recommended and developed.** |
| Quiet Grid | A typographic, editorial direction with a strict 8-column grid and a square `8` monogram. | Held back: feels more like a software studio than a device-resale business. |
| Signal Thread | A fine, continuous line passing through the letters to suggest a device’s ongoing story. | Held back: elegant at large sizes but too fragile for favicon and header use. |

## 2. Positioning, personality and voice

### Positioning

D8D Tech is a proposed used-phone resale business **launching in Ireland**, focused on a premium pre-owned smartphone experience for retail and wholesale partners. The brand should speak with confidence about the quality-led standard it is building while avoiding unsupported guarantees, completed-certification claims or invented operating details.

### Intended qualities—not proven promises

| Intended value | Say it like this | Do not turn it into |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Care | “A considered next chapter for useful devices.” | A guarantee of condition, warranty or repair quality. |
| Clarity | “A clearer route to quality pre-owned smartphones.” | A claim of certified testing, standardised grading or compliance. |
| Renewal | “A device can have a next chapter.” | Quantified environmental impact, “zero waste” or a recycling claim. |
| Honesty | “Launching soon in Ireland.” | A polished substitute for real launch, trading or supply information. |

### Voice

- **Clear and human:** short, unhurried sentences; concrete words before technical terms.
- **Quietly assured:** avoid hype, urgency, exclamation-heavy copy and exaggerated luxury language.
- **Confident about direction:** use “built for,” “designed for,” “our standard” and “launching soon” where factual; retain appropriate qualification for unverified promises.
- **Respectful of personal data:** refer to data erasure only as an intended process unless a verified procedure exists.
- **Not overtly religious:** “new beginning,” “next chapter” and “Day Eight” are enough; do not add faith-specific statements without separate owner direction.

### Copy examples

- **Recommended pre-launch line:** “A sharper standard for smartphone resale.”
- **Safe status line:** “Launching soon in Ireland.”
- **Safe explanatory line:** “A clearer route to quality pre-owned smartphones.”
- **Avoid:** “Ireland’s trusted refurbished specialists”; “fully tested and guaranteed”; “saving the planet one phone at a time”; “shop now.”

All SVGs contain path data only—no remote font dependency or embedded script. The original letterforms are deliberately geometric and should not be recreated with a system font.

### Clear space

Keep clear space equal to the **height of the teal `8` counter** around all sides of the full mark. For the compact mark or standalone symbol, keep clear space equal to **one quarter of the mark height**. Clear space is a minimum, not a target.

### Minimum sizes

| Asset | Digital minimum | Print minimum |
| --- | ---:| ---:|
| Full wordmark | 132 px wide | 35 mm wide |
| Compact D8D mark | 64 px wide | 16 mm wide |
| Standalone `8` symbol | 24 px high | 6 mm high |
| Favicon | 16 px square | N/A |

Below these sizes, use the supplied favicon rather than redrawing or simplifying the mark.

### Backgrounds and misuse

**Use:**

- Full-colour wordmark on **Paper**, white or very light neutral fields.
- Light-on-dark version on **Night** and sufficiently dark photographic/graphic fields.
- One-colour mark only where a single ink is a real production constraint.
- Favicon on browser chrome, pinned tabs and small digital contexts.

**Do not:**

- Alter the D/8/D proportions or the wordmark letter spacing.
- Recolour the `8` independently of the approved palette.
- Place the dark logo on low-contrast photographic imagery.
- Add gradients, outlines, shadows, bevels, registration symbols or slogans to the mark.
- Use the `8` alone as a substitute for the name where the brand is not already clearly established.
- Rotate, crop, stretch or recreate it using a similar typeface.

## 5. Typography

The website uses a resilient, no-download system stack so it makes no external font request:

- **Display:** `Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif` — editorial warmth, used for main messages and supports the “new chapter” tone.
- **Interface/body:** `Inter, ui-sans-serif, system-ui, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", sans-serif` — a compact, Apple-adjacent system interface rhythm. If Inter is unavailable, the host operating system’s UI type is used.

The custom D8D wordmark is path artwork, not live type. Use normal text for all other copy. Do not purchase, package or imply a licence for a third-party font through this prototype.

| Level | Usage | Website treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display 1 | Hero | Georgia, regular, 56–94 px, tight tracking. |
| Display 2 | Section titles | Georgia, regular, 42–75 px, tight tracking. |
| Heading 3 | Cards/labels | System sans, 17–19 px, semibold. |
| Body | Main explanatory copy | System sans, 16–18 px, 1.6–1.65 line-height. |
| Eyebrow | Labels/status | System sans, 11–12 px, 700+, tracked uppercase. |

## 6. Layout, components and motion

### Tokens used by the implementation

The following are defined in `client/src/index.css` and should remain the single source of truth for the site:

```css
--ink: #0D1B2A;
--night: #071522;
--paper: #F5F6F3;
--paper-warm: #ECEFE9;
--mint: #30BFA5;
--mint-pale: #D6F2EB;
--gold: #E8B85C;
--ink-soft: #344255;
--radius-sm: 12px;
--radius-md: 20px;
--radius-lg: 32px;
--radius-xl: 48px;
--shadow-soft: 0 18px 60px rgba(7, 21, 34, 0.09);
--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1);
```

- **Content width:** 1320 px maximum for wide sections; use 5–6.5vw side gutters and maintain at least 20 px on very narrow screens.
- **Spacing:** quiet and generous. Major section blocks use 82–156 px vertical rhythm; component internals generally use 10, 16, 20, 24 or 32 px.
- **Corners:** 12 px for compact utility elements; 20 px for cards; 32–48 px for large feature surfaces.
- **Borders:** use low-contrast Ink or white at 9–18% opacity; never substitute a heavy border for hierarchy.
- **Iconography:** simple 1.8–2 px line icons, no product-brand logos, with a visible text label unless the familiar control has an accessible name.

### Controls

- **Primary button:** Ink text on Mint, pill-shaped, 48–50 px minimum height, visible focus ring in Gold.
- **Text link:** semibold label with a small direction arrow; animated underline only on hover/focus.
- **Navigation:** simple, visible text links; mobile menu is a real toggle button with `aria-expanded` and close-on-navigation behaviour.
- **Focus:** a 3 px Gold outline with 4 px offset is present for keyboard navigation.

### Motion

Motion is a small confirmation, not decoration. Use transform/opacity transitions under 300 ms with `--ease-out`; buttons may scale to 0.97 on active. No critical information is conveyed by motion. The implementation removes nonessential motion when `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is active.

## 7. Imagery and asset rules

- The hero device is a **CSS illustration**, not a photograph of owned inventory. It must remain clearly decorative and not be labelled as product stock.
- Prefer original vector abstractions, close material details, controlled gradients and generous blank space over stock photos of devices, warehouses or people.
- Avoid generic recycling marks, green leaves, world maps, hands holding phones, device-brand logos and imagery that could imply an approved supply chain.
- Any future photo or external visual requires a documented source, licence, attribution requirement, modification note and confirmation that it does not suggest unverified operations.
- Do not upload owner information, private correspondence or third-party device data to a visual-generation service.

See [`ASSET-LICENSES.md`](ASSET-LICENSES.md) for the current provenance record, and open [`brand/preview/index.html`](brand/preview/index.html) locally for the visual reference board.

## 8. Review gates before public use

The owner must separately approve the final name/identity, resolve rights and company/trader status, decide the correct legal/contact/privacy disclosures, and authorise a domain, hosting and publication. A future sales journey, customer-data collection or any operational promise requires separately scoped work and factual review.
